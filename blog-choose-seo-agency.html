How to Choose an SEO Agency Based on Deliverables, Reporting and Business Goals

The right SEO partner is not the cheapest or the loudest — it is the one whose work, reporting and goals match yours.

MT

Miaoon Strategy Team

Nov 3, 2026 · 9 min read

Summary
  • Choosing an SEO agency requires more than comparing monthly fees or ranking promises.
  • Businesses should understand the actual work, reporting process, and strategy behind the proposal.
  • Clear deliverables help companies know what they are paying for and how progress will be reviewed.
  • The right SEO partner should connect website improvements with meaningful business goals.

Choosing an SEO agency can be confusing, especially when different companies offer different packages, timelines, and promises. One agency may focus on backlinks, another may offer technical audits, while another may promise a fixed number of blogs every month.

But how do you know which one is right for your business?

The answer is to look beyond the package price. You should understand what the agency will actually deliver, how it will report progress, and whether its work supports your business goals.

At Miaoon, SEO should be viewed as a business growth activity rather than a checklist of tasks. The strategy needs to make sense for the website, audience, and outcomes a company wants to achieve.

1. Understand What the Agency Will Actually Deliver

Before signing an agreement, ask for a clear list of deliverables. A proposal should explain the work included in the service rather than relying on broad phrases such as "complete SEO" or "full website optimisation".

Technical SEO Work

Technical SEO may include website crawling, indexing checks, redirect reviews, site structure analysis, mobile usability, and performance recommendations.

Ask whether the agency will only identify issues or also help implement fixes. Google's SEO starter guide provides useful background on website accessibility and search fundamentals.

On-Page SEO

On-page work may include title tags, meta descriptions, headings, content improvements, image alt text, and internal linking.

The agency should explain which pages it will optimise and why. Google's guide to title links explains how page titles can help users understand search results.

Content Strategy

Content deliverables may include keyword research, content calendars, blog writing, service-page improvements, and content refreshes.

Ask whether the agency researches search intent and business relevance before recommending topics. Google's guidance on helpful content is a useful reference for evaluating content quality.

Off-Page SEO

Off-page SEO may involve digital PR, relevant outreach, brand mentions, and backlink analysis. Ask how links are acquired and how the agency evaluates source quality.

Google's link spam policies explain practices that can violate its guidelines. Be cautious of agencies that sell guaranteed quantities of manipulative links.

2. Check Whether the Agency Understands Your Business Goals

SEO should support what the business is trying to achieve. A local clinic may want appointment enquiries, while an ecommerce store may focus on product sales. A B2B company may need qualified leads rather than a large number of general visitors.

Before choosing an agency, discuss:

  • Your main products or services.
  • Your target customers and locations.
  • Your strongest competitors.
  • Your most valuable website pages.
  • The actions you want visitors to take.
  • Your current challenges and growth priorities.

The agency should use this information to recommend a strategy. It should not offer exactly the same plan to every business.

3. Evaluate the Reporting Process

A good SEO report should explain what happened, why it matters, and what the agency plans to do next.

Organic Traffic and Search Visibility

Ask for reports covering organic clicks, impressions, search queries, landing pages, and relevant ranking trends.

Google Search Console is an important source for understanding how a website performs in Google Search. It helps businesses review search visibility and identify technical or indexing issues.

Leads and Conversions

Traffic alone does not explain whether SEO is helping the business. Ask how the agency will track enquiries, calls, form submissions, purchases, or other meaningful actions.

Google Analytics can help businesses understand website activity and user behaviour. For more detailed measurement, review the GA4 event guide.

Clear Explanations and Next Steps

A report should not be a spreadsheet filled with numbers that nobody explains. It should highlight progress, problems, completed work, and upcoming priorities.

Tools such as Looker Studio can help present performance data in dashboards. The agency should still explain what the figures mean for the business.

4. Ask About the Tools and Working Process

SEO agencies use different tools depending on their services and workflows. You do not need to insist on one particular platform, but you should understand how the agency researches, audits, and measures its work.

For example, Semrush and Ahrefs offer tools for keyword research, competitor analysis, and backlink research. Screaming Frog can help with technical website crawling.

Ask which tools will be used, what data you will receive, and whether you will have access to your own accounts and reports.

5. Review the Agency's Experience and Communication

An agency should be able to explain its process in language you understand. Ask for examples of relevant work, the type of businesses it has supported, and how it handles challenges.

You can also review the agency's public presence through platforms such as LinkedIn and its website. Treat testimonials and case studies as supporting information, not as guaranteed predictions of your results.

Pay attention to communication. Find out who will manage your account, how often you will meet, and how questions or urgent issues will be handled.

6. Understand Pricing, Timelines, and Contract Terms

SEO is an ongoing process, and results depend on competition, website condition, content quality, and many other factors. Be careful with promises of guaranteed first-page rankings or instant traffic growth.

Before agreeing, check:

  • Monthly fees and any additional charges.
  • Exact deliverables and their frequency.
  • Contract length and cancellation terms.
  • Ownership of content and website assets.
  • Access to analytics and SEO accounts.
  • Reporting frequency and meeting schedule.
  • Who is responsible for implementing website changes.

A transparent agency should explain what is included and what falls outside the agreement.

The most useful way to compare agencies is to connect their proposed work with your business priorities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should an SEO agency include in its monthly deliverables?
Deliverables depend on the website and business goals. They may include technical SEO reviews, keyword research, on-page optimisation, content planning, content updates, internal linking, and performance reporting. The proposal should clearly state what is included.

How do I know if an SEO agency is doing good work?
Review completed tasks, technical improvements, content quality, search visibility, organic traffic, and business conversions. Ask the agency to explain progress and the next steps rather than relying only on ranking screenshots.

Should I choose an SEO agency that guarantees first-page rankings?
Be cautious. No agency can control Google's ranking systems or guarantee a specific position for every keyword. Look for a realistic strategy, transparent reporting, and clear deliverables instead.

How long should I give an SEO agency to show results?
SEO timelines vary based on competition, website condition, industry, and the work involved. Ask the agency for realistic milestones and regular progress reviews rather than expecting a guaranteed result by a fixed date.

Is cheaper SEO always a bad choice?
Not necessarily. Compare the actual work, expertise, communication, and reporting behind the price. A low-cost package may be suitable for limited needs, but it should not promise extensive work that it cannot realistically deliver.

Conclusion

Choosing an SEO agency is not simply about finding the lowest price or the biggest list of services. Businesses should compare clear deliverables, useful reporting, relevant experience, communication, and the connection between SEO activities and business goals.
