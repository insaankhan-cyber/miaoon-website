SaaS is Dead (taking its last breath), GaaS is a la mode!

SaaS is dead as the default way for an agency to get work done, because clients and teams now want finished output instead of more software to operate. GaaS, or Agentic as a Service, delivers that output through managed AI agents. These agents pursue a defined goal, use your connected tools, and return a result or an exception for review. SaaS still stores your files, briefs, and reports. However, GaaS now does the clicking. At Miaoon, we see this shift as the biggest change to agency operations since the cloud.

Summary

  1. SaaS gives your team software to operate. GaaS gives your team managed agents that complete bounded work.
  2. SaaS is dead as an operating model, but it lives on as the system of record.
  3. GaaS sits on top of your existing tools and uses them on your behalf.
  4. Agencies gain the most from GaaS in repeatable, multi-step work such as reporting, resizing, and approval chasing.
  5. The safest start is one bounded task with clear human review.

SaaS is dead as an operating model, not as a system of record

SaaS changed how software reaches agencies. Instead of installing tools on every laptop, you rent hosted ones. That was a real shift. However, your team still learns each tool, configures it, and operates it every day.

Because of this, SaaS never changed who does the work. A producer still opens the project board, updates statuses, and chases feedback. A designer still exports, renames, and uploads files. The subscription moved to the cloud, but the labour stayed with your people.

That is why SaaS is dead in its role as the thing you operate. Your project tool, asset library, and analytics dashboard will keep storing records. Nevertheless, the daily effort of pushing work through those screens is moving to agents.

GaaS delivers managed execution toward a goal

GaaS is a service model in which managed AI agents perform bounded work for users or systems. You supply the goal, the approved data, the business rules, and the accountable reviewers. The provider runs the models, the tool connections, the monitoring, and the approval steps.

Consider a typical client reporting cycle. With SaaS, an account manager pulls numbers from three platforms, pastes them into a deck, writes commentary, and updates the project board. With GaaS, the team states one goal: collect last week’s campaign data, draft a source-based summary, and flag any metric that moved more than 20%. The agent completes the routine steps, and the account manager reviews the flagged items.

As a result, the human role changes. People stop operating tools and start setting goals, defining limits, and reviewing important decisions.

Clients and teams choose outcomes over tools

Buyers have always wanted results more than tools. Nobody hires an agency to admire its software stack. They hire it to grow a brand. Agentic AI finally makes it practical to deliver that result with less bloat in between.

The numbers show why services lead the economy. Services make up over 72% of U.S. GDP, and the software market is following that pull. Investors have noticed too, since VC-backed companies raised more than $80 billion in the first quarter of 2025, with software leading.

Vendors already respond to this demand. Zendesk now sells automated customer service where AI resolves tickets end to end. Amplitude launched agents that recommend growth actions and even set up A/B tests. In other words, these companies sell outcomes through a product wrapper. The Forbes analysis by Alexander Puutio calls this shift “servicification”. For agencies, the lesson is direct: clients will compare your speed and output, not your tool list.

GaaS operates SaaS instead of replacing it

GaaS does not erase SaaS. Agents still need systems where files live and actions land. For example, a GaaS workflow can read a brief, create tasks, update a status, and save approved files inside your existing software.

That means SaaS stays the system of record. GaaS becomes the execution layer across those systems. Feluda’s explainer on GaaS vs SaaS describes the same split: SaaS provides software capabilities, while GaaS provides managed execution toward a goal.

Many SaaS products also add AI features, such as text generation and chat assistants. However, those features do not make a product agentic. A product becomes GaaS only when the system pursues a goal through actions and observations within approved boundaries.

Agency work that GaaS can take over first

Not every task suits an agent. The best candidates are repeatable, span several tools, and have a result you can check. Here is where agencies usually see early gains.

Turns briefs into organised tasks

An agent reads a new client brief, extracts deliverables and deadlines, and creates the tasks in your project tool. Your producer then reviews the plan instead of typing it.

Prepares client reports

The agent gathers campaign data from approved sources and drafts a summary with the numbers linked to their origin. Consequently, your account team spends its time on insight instead of copy and paste.

Resizes and organises assets

Routine versioning, naming, and filing follow clear rules. An agent handles the volume, while a designer spot-checks the output.

Chases approvals and feedback

The agent sends reminders when a client goes quiet and logs the replies against the right project. Your timelines hold even when your team is busy.

Escalates creative and sensitive calls

Brand tone, concept direction, and pricing stay with people. When the agent meets an unclear or sensitive case, it stops and sends the case to a human. Therefore, creative judgment remains yours.

SaaS still wins in some situations

Not every process needs an agent. SaaS remains the better choice when the process is simple, when your team needs direct control, or when a clear interface already solves the problem. It also wins when mistakes would be hard to detect or when you cannot yet monitor agent actions.

In addition, deterministic features often beat an agent on reliability. A fixed rule that runs the same way every time is easier to trust than a plan an agent builds at run time. So the honest answer for most agencies is a hybrid: SaaS for records and control, GaaS for repeatable work that crosses tools.

How to adopt GaaS without losing control

Start small and keep boundaries tight. Follow these steps.

  1. Pick one repeatable task with a measurable result, such as weekly client reporting.
  2. List the data sources the agent may read and the systems it may write to.
  3. Set approval rules for sensitive actions, such as anything a client will see.
  4. Name an owner who reviews exceptions every day.
  5. Track completed tasks, exception rate, correction rate, and time saved per accepted outcome.
  6. Expand to the next task only after the first one performs well.

Importantly, high agent activity does not prove useful work. Measure accepted outcomes, not the number of runs. Also, protect client data by limiting what each agent can send and where it can send it.

Conclusion

SaaS gave agencies tools they could use. GaaS gives them managed agents that use those tools toward a defined outcome. SaaS is dead as the thing your team operates all day, yet it lives on as the place where your files and records sit.

The better question is not whether GaaS will eliminate SaaS. Instead, ask which work your people should keep doing directly and which bounded tasks agents can handle safely. Agencies that answer this early will ship faster, protect creative time, and spend less effort on routine clicks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SaaS really dead?

SaaS is dead as the main way people do work, but not as a technology. Agencies still need hosted tools to store files and run actions. What changes is who operates them: agents handle more of the routine steps.

What does GaaS stand for?

GaaS stands for Agentic as a Service. It is a service model where managed AI agents perform bounded work toward a goal, using software and tools on your behalf.

What is the difference between SaaS and GaaS?

SaaS provides software that people operate. GaaS provides managed agents that operate software to deliver an outcome. SaaS tends to use seat-based pricing, while GaaS may add task, execution, or outcome-based pricing.

Does GaaS replace my agency’s current tools?

No. GaaS works on top of your existing tools. Your project board, asset library, and analytics platforms remain the systems of record, and agents use them to complete tasks.

Is an AI chatbot the same as GaaS?

No. A chatbot answers questions. A GaaS agent receives a goal, takes several approved steps, uses connected systems, and escalates cases it cannot resolve.

Can GaaS replace creatives?

No. GaaS handles repeatable operations such as reporting, filing, and reminders. Concepts, brand voice, and client relationships still need human judgment.

Which agency tasks should we automate first?

Start with repeatable, multi-step work that has a checkable result, such as client reporting, asset resizing, or approval follow-ups.
