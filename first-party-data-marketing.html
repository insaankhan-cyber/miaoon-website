First-Party Data Marketing: How Businesses Can Grow Without Depending on Third-Party Cookies

Direct customer data is the foundation of better marketing — here is how to collect it responsibly and put it to work.

  • Website forms, subscriptions, purchases and customer feedback can support more relevant marketing.

Every business wants to understand its customers better. Knowing what people are interested in, which products they explore and what influences their buying decisions can help brands create more relevant marketing campaigns.

However, customer privacy expectations and changes in digital advertising technology have made data collection more important than ever. Businesses can no longer build their entire marketing strategy around assumptions about external tracking.

This is where first-party data marketing becomes valuable.

At Miaoon, we see customer data as an important part of understanding marketing performance. When businesses collect and use information responsibly, they can create better customer experiences, improve campaign planning and make more informed marketing decisions.

1. Why First-Party Data Marketing Matters for Businesses

First-party data is information a business collects directly through its own interactions with customers and website visitors.

This can include:

  • Details submitted through website enquiry forms.
  • Customer purchase and transaction history.
  • Newsletter subscriptions.
  • Product preferences.
  • Customer feedback and service requests.
  • Information shared through loyalty programmes.

For example, an ecommerce business can understand which products customers purchase frequently. A coaching institute can organise course enquiries based on the programmes students are interested in.

Google's Privacy Sandbox updates are an important reminder that businesses should keep up with changes in advertising technology instead of depending on outdated assumptions about browser tracking.

In April 2025, Google announced that it would maintain its existing approach to third-party cookie choices in Chrome rather than introducing a new standalone prompt.

Source: Google Privacy Sandbox update.

For businesses, the bigger opportunity is to build direct customer relationships and improve how marketing information is used.

2.1 Website Forms That Generate Relevant Leads

A website enquiry form is often one of the first direct interactions between a business and a potential customer.

However, a form should not simply collect as much information as possible. It should make it easy for visitors to share the details needed for the next step.

For example, a service business may ask for a visitor's name, contact information and a short description of the requirement.

Miaoon can help businesses improve their website content and enquiry journey so that forms communicate the value of the service clearly and support better lead generation.

Useful references include the ICO's guidance on collecting information, W3C Web Accessibility Initiative and Google's website usability guidance.

2.2 CRM and Customer Journey Management

When customer information is scattered across spreadsheets, emails and messaging applications, it becomes difficult to understand the complete customer journey.

A CRM system can help businesses organise enquiries, interactions, follow-ups and sales activities.

Miaoon can help businesses think about how their digital campaigns and website enquiries connect with the customer journey. This is particularly useful for businesses that receive leads through multiple channels, such as paid advertisements, organic search and social media.

Businesses can explore HubSpot CRM, Salesforce CRM, Google Analytics and Google Tag Manager as part of their broader marketing and measurement setup.

2.3 Email Marketing That Builds Customer Relationships

Email marketing gives businesses an opportunity to communicate directly with people who have chosen to receive updates.

Instead of sending the same message to everyone, brands can organise communication around customer interests and relevant business updates.

For example, an education company can share course information with interested students, while an ecommerce brand can communicate product updates with subscribers.

Miaoon's content and marketing approach can support the planning of relevant messages, campaign communication and customer engagement.

Businesses should also follow applicable email marketing rules and provide clear unsubscribe options. Helpful resources include Google's email sender guidelines, Mailchimp's email marketing guide and the ICO's direct marketing guidance.

Customer questions, product interests and feedback can help businesses identify topics that deserve attention.

Miaoon can use these insights to inform content strategy, website communication and paid campaign messaging.

4. Responsible Data Collection and Customer Privacy

First-party data marketing should always be built around transparency and responsible handling of information.

Businesses should:

  • Explain why information is being collected.
  • Collect only what is genuinely required.
  • Provide clear privacy information.
  • Use appropriate security controls.
  • Limit access to authorised people.
  • Respect applicable consent and communication requirements.

For businesses operating in India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is an important legal reference. The exact obligations should be assessed against applicable laws and rules.

Additional resources include the ICO's consent guidance, FTC privacy and security guidance, CERT-In and OWASP.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is first-party data marketing?
First-party data marketing involves using information collected directly from a business's own customers or audience to improve marketing communication and customer experiences.

How can small businesses collect first-party data?
Small businesses can begin with website enquiry forms, customer feedback, newsletter subscriptions, purchase records and CRM systems.

Is first-party data useful for digital advertising?
Yes. When collected and used appropriately, first-party data can help businesses understand their audiences and make better-informed campaign decisions. Its use must follow applicable platform policies and privacy laws.
