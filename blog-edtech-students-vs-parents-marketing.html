Why EdTech Companies Need Different Marketing Strategies for Students and Parents

Students and parents influence the enrolment journey in different ways — here is how to speak to both without diluting your message.

MT

Miaoon Strategy Team

Nov 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Summary
  • Students and parents often have different expectations when choosing an online learning platform.
  • Students look for engaging learning experiences, while parents focus on quality, safety, and value.
  • EdTech companies need separate messages that address both audiences.
  • A well-planned marketing strategy can build trust and support better student enrolments.

EdTech companies often market their courses, learning apps, and online programmes to students. However, students are not always the people making the final purchase decision. In many cases, parents research the options, compare fees, and decide whether a course is worth the investment.

This creates a unique marketing challenge. Students want to know whether a platform makes learning interesting and convenient, while parents want confidence that the programme offers genuine educational value. Understand What Students and Parents Actually Want

Students and parents may be interested in the same course, but their priorities are often different.

Students may look for:

  • Easy-to-understand lessons and engaging content.
  • Flexible learning schedules.
  • Practice tests, quizzes, and progress tracking.
  • Interactive sessions and doubt-solving support.
  • Learning experiences that match their goals.

Parents may focus on:

  • Teacher qualifications and course quality.
  • Learning outcomes and progress reports.
  • Course fees and payment options.
  • Student support and safety.
  • Credibility, reviews, and transparency.

EdTech companies should research both audiences before creating advertisements or website content. The UNESCO education resources provide useful context on education and learning priorities. The OECD education reports also offer insights into education systems and student outcomes.

Create Different Messages for Each Audience

Using the same message for students and parents can make marketing less effective. A student-focused campaign might highlight interactive classes, engaging lessons, and flexible learning. A parent-focused campaign should explain teaching methods, faculty experience, course structure, and progress monitoring.

For example, an advertisement for a coding course could show students building a project, while a parent-focused landing page could explain the curriculum, mentor support, and learning milestones.

Use Google Ads and Meta Business to plan audience-specific campaigns. The Google Ads education resources can help marketers understand campaign setup and targeting options.

Use Content That Answers Real Questions

Parents and students usually research before enrolling. They may search for course comparisons, learning methods, faculty information, fees, study schedules, and career opportunities.

Create separate content for these needs. Student-focused content can include course previews, short tutorials, interactive challenges, and student stories. Parent-focused content can include detailed course guides, fee explanations, learning support information, and progress-report examples.

Publish videos through YouTube and use YouTube education resources to explore educational content formats. You can also review Google Trends to understand interest in relevant learning topics.

For website content, Google's SEO starter guide offers useful guidance on making pages accessible to search engines. Its helpful content guidance encourages businesses to create information that genuinely helps readers.

Build Trust Through Transparency

Parents are careful about where they invest their children's time and money. EdTech companies should avoid exaggerated claims about guaranteed marks, placements, or career outcomes.

Clearly explain course duration, fees, refund policies, class formats, faculty details, and available support. Share authentic testimonials only with permission and provide enough context so that prospective customers can evaluate them fairly.

For businesses serving younger learners, privacy and safety deserve particular attention. Review the UNICEF child online safety resources and the UNESCO digital learning guidance when developing responsible learning experiences.

The U.S. FTC education guidance is also a useful reference for understanding truthful marketing and consumer protection principles. Businesses operating in India should review applicable Indian laws and policies rather than assuming foreign rules apply directly.

Make Enquiry and Admission Follow-Up Easier

An interested student may enquire through a website form, social media, a phone call, or a webinar. If the institution does not respond promptly, the student may choose another platform.

Use a structured process to record enquiries, identify course interests, schedule counselling, and follow up with relevant information. Keep students and parents informed about the next steps without sending unnecessary messages.

A CRM or admission management system can help teams organise enquiries and track admission progress. Use Google Analytics to understand website activity and Google Search Console to monitor organic search performance.

Measure Results Separately for Both Audiences

Do not measure every campaign only by clicks or total leads. Track student engagement, parent enquiries, counselling bookings, trial-class attendance, application completion, and paid enrolments.

Compare which messages and channels bring the most relevant enquiries. This helps the marketing team improve campaigns over time.

FAQs

1. Why should EdTech companies target parents and students separately?
Their priorities are different. Students often focus on learning experience and flexibility, while parents may prioritise quality, safety, fees, and outcomes.

2. Which marketing channels work well for EdTech companies?
SEO, search advertising, social media, educational videos, webinars, email, and counselling campaigns can all support student acquisition.

3. How can EdTech companies build trust with parents?
Share transparent course details, qualified faculty information, realistic learning expectations, genuine reviews, and clear student support policies.

4. What should EdTech companies measure?
Track qualified enquiries, counselling attendance, trial participation, application completion, enrolments, and student retention.

Conclusion

EdTech companies need to communicate with students and parents differently because both audiences influence the enrolment journey in different ways. Student-focused messaging should highlight learning experience, while parent-focused communication should establish trust through transparency and educational value. When both approaches work together, marketing becomes more relevant and can support better enrolment decisions.
